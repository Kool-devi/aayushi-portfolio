TL;DR

Attune supports couples in different moments — from private reflection to conflict, repair, and long-term growth.

I worked across the product to define a clearer interaction model, create stronger privacy and consent boundaries, simplify complex shared flows, and build a reusable system that could survive a major development-team change.

The biggest lesson was that designing AI was less about what it should say and more about what role it should have in each moment.

Overview

Attune is an AI-powered relationship app for couples.

I joined after the main product and modules had already been defined. My role was to help turn those pieces into one clear experience across the relationship modes, assessments, safety flows, shared sessions, and design system.

The challenge was not adding more features. It was making very different emotional moments feel like they belonged to the same product.

The problem

Attune already had a lot of functionality.

But a private conversation with AI, a conflict between two partners, a relationship assessment, and a crisis moment cannot all behave the same way. We needed to answer three questions:

Question 01

What does the user need in this moment?

Question 02

Is this experience private or shared?

Question 03

What role should AI play here?

Without those answers, the product risked feeling like the same chatbot repeated across different screens.

Goals

Make the product easier to understand

Help users quickly know which experience fits what they need.

Design AI around context

Change the role of AI depending on whether someone is reflecting alone, reconnecting, or talking with a partner.

Build trust

Make privacy, consent, safety, and shared information clear throughout the experience.

Design process

Define the product model

Before designing the visuals, I needed to make a structural design decision.

I defined what kind of relationship support each mode represented and whether it belonged to one person or to the couple. This private/shared distinction became the foundation for the rest of the product.

Private Shared

Reflect

I need to understand what I am feeling.

Repair

I need to calm down and process what happened.

Resolve

We need to work through something together.

Refine

We want to keep strengthening the relationship.

This helped establish a simple product model:

Private self-work → Shared couple-work

The modes are connected, but they are not a required sequence.

Make the modes feel connected

A simple solution would have been four cards. I moved away from that because it made the modes feel like separate features.

Instead, I designed them as full-screen spaces users could move between. Each mode has its own mood, copy, and visual treatment, while the same navigation keeps them connected.

Why

The interface needed to communicate a change in emotional context, not just a change in feature.

The four modes

01 / 04 · Private

Reflect

Reflect is a private space where users can talk through what they are feeling before deciding whether to act on it or involve their partner.

I kept the layout close to familiar AI chat patterns, with text, voice, and starter prompts all easy to access. That reduces learning and keeps the focus on processing the issue itself.

Design decision

Use a familiar chat model and keep the flow lightweight so users can start reflecting quickly.

01 Reflect Prototype placeholder
02 / 04 · Private

Repair

Repair is for after conflict, when the user may still feel activated and is not ready to reconnect yet.

The flow starts with a body check-in and short grounding exercise, then moves into AI-guided reflection and optional preparation for reconnecting with a partner.

Design decision

Slow the flow down and keep every shared step explicit, so the user controls when private reflection becomes a partner conversation.

03 / 04 · Shared

Resolve

Resolve is a shared mode for couples working through an active conflict with AI guiding the conversation.

It starts with mutual consent and shared ground rules, then uses turn-taking, protected speaking time, and AI clarification checks to keep the discussion balanced.

Design decision

Structure the conversation around consent, equal speaking time, and clarification so both partners have a fair way to be heard.

04 / 04 · Shared

Refine

Refine is for couples who want to keep working on their relationship even when there is no active conflict.

Unlike Resolve, it is more open-ended. Couples can schedule a session, repeat it over time, and work through guided prompts or small activities together. I also added light AI interventions, like appreciation prompts and focus warnings, to keep the conversation useful without taking it over.

If the conversation starts becoming too intense, the app can suggest moving into Repair instead.

Design decision

Make Refine feel flexible and repeatable, with enough guidance to support growth without turning every session into conflict resolution.

Turning points

Turning point 01

When I realised the safest AI action was sometimes to stop

One of the hardest questions came from the Safety flow. What should happen if someone says something that is bigger than a relationship app should handle?

Instead of asking the AI to keep the conversation going, we designed it to pause the session, check on the user, and surface real-world crisis support.

The standalone Safety area also gives users access to region-aware resources and makes it clear that Attune is not a crisis service.

Why this mattered to me

Responsible AI is not only about giving better answers. Sometimes the responsible decision is knowing when the product should step aside.

Turning point 02

When the development team changed mid-project

Partway through the project, the client replaced the development team and the handoff timeline suddenly became much tighter.

That changed the importance of the design system. What had started as good organisation became essential.

We went screen by screen, identified repeating elements, checked whether a component already existed, and used variants instead of creating duplicates.

We also organised the product module by module so a new team could understand what to build without reopening every design decision. In total, the handoff covered roughly 169 screens.

Why

A design system is not just about consistency. It is also how design knowledge survives when the people building the product change.

The part I cared about most

Designing where AI should stop

The most important design decision was not about what AI should say. It was about knowing when AI should no longer be the right support.

Attune includes a safety layer that can pause a live session when something serious is disclosed, surface region-aware crisis resources, and guide the user toward real-world help.

The user can continue when they are ready, but the product does not try to keep them engaged at all costs.

That principle shaped a lot of the wider experience:

  • Private conversations stay private.
  • Partners consent before shared sessions.
  • Difficult conversations have structure.
  • Sessions can pause instead of forcing resolution.
  • Real-world support takes priority when needed.

Designing the modes

Each mode uses the same product language, then changes its pacing and interaction rules to fit the moment.

02
Private · after conflict

Repair slows the moment down

Repair begins with a body check-in, moves through grounding, then opens into AI-guided processing and an optional invitation to reconnect with a partner. In a charged moment, fewer choices help the experience feel supportive rather than clinical or task-driven.

Shared · active conflict

Resolve structures a difficult conversation

Resolve is a shared mode where both partners work through an active conflict with AI facilitating. The interaction needed to support fairness, consent, pacing, and context from both sides.

AIFacilitator
BPartner B
  • Both partners consent before the session begins.
  • Turn-taking gives each person uninterrupted speaking time.
  • Prompts adapt to what both people have already said.
  • The session can pause or end without forcing a resolution.
  • Summaries and follow-up actions remain consent-based.
04
Shared · ongoing growth

Refine is designed for repeat use

Refine supports couples who are not in active conflict but want to keep working on the relationship. A one-off “start now” action was not enough, so the experience became a sustainable shared ritual.

  2. 02Repeat weekly

Personalization & safety

Personalization needed to feel useful without becoming invisible or intrusive.

Assessments

Personalize the wider product

Attachment style, love language, conflict style, emotional sensitivities, and relationship history can improve later experiences. The value is not the quiz—it is how those results quietly shape future support.

  1. Intro
  2. Questions
  3. AI profile
  4. Result
  5. Consent for joint use
Anything shared between partners stays explicit and optional.
Safety

Know when AI should stop

Safety exists both as a standalone space and inside live sessions. When a disclosure is bigger than the product should handle, the AI pauses and directs the user toward appropriate real-world support.

  1. Sensitive disclosure
  2. Pause
  3. Check danger
  4. Surface local help
  5. Continue only if ready
Choose restraint over engagement.

Designing the system

The work had to survive a major implementation change—not just look complete.

Midway through the project, budget pressure led to a full development-team change. Documentation became a hard delivery requirement, and system thinking became the difference between a collection of screens and a product another team could continue building.

~169 frames organized into module-level prototypes and documentation

Key iterations

Session cards

Made ownership clearer

Replaced long labels with partner identity and “Scheduled by [Name]”.

Refine scheduling

Supported long-term use

Expanded one-off actions into repeat, reminder, reschedule, and notification states.

Assessment results

Protected privacy

Replaced manual sharing with consent-based use inside joint sessions.

Motion workflow

Protected tone and accuracy

Locked accurate screens first, then added motion to priority transitions.

Component process

Reduced duplication

Moved from ad-hoc creation to a screen audit and variant-first approach.

Outcome

The project moved from a set of existing modules toward a more coherent product system.

The final direction created:

  • Clearer roles for each relationship mode.
  • Stronger private vs. shared boundaries.
  • Different AI behaviours for different emotional contexts.
  • Explicit consent in sensitive flows.
  • A more useful Safety layer.
  • Repeatable patterns across the product.
  • A cleaner system for development and handoff.

The strongest outcome was not one screen. It was making the product feel consistent without making every experience feel the same.

What I learned

Attune changed the way I think about AI products.

At the start, it was easy to focus on questions like:

What should the AI say?

By the end, the more useful questions were:

Why is AI here?

What should it know?

What should stay private?

When should it stop?

The shift from designing AI responses to designing AI boundaries was the most important thing I took from the project.
