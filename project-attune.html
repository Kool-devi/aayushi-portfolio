TL;DR

I worked across the full mobile experience, from product architecture and interaction design to live AI sessions, assessments, scheduling, system consistency, prototyping, and developer handoff.

Overview

Attune is a relationship app for committed couples who want support before, during, or after difficult conversations.

The product was already built when I joined. Its core functionality existed, but the experience felt fragmented across modes, assessments, safety, and shared sessions. I focused on giving that existing product a clear design direction and an interaction model that felt consistent, emotionally appropriate, and buildable.

The problem

Multiple emotionally different experiences needed to feel like one product—but not like the same AI chat.

A private reflection, a conflict-resolution session, a relationship assessment, and a crisis-safety moment each need different behaviours. At the same time, couples need to understand how those experiences relate and when each one is appropriate.

Who we designed for

Serious couples—living together or long-distance—who need both individual self-work and shared support.

The design question

How might one product support reflection, repair, conflict, growth, personalization, and safety without treating every need the same way?

Defining the product model

The first useful design decision was structural, not visual.

I defined what kind of relationship support each mode represented and whether it belonged to one person or to the couple. This private/shared distinction became the foundation for the rest of the product.

Designing the modes

Each mode uses the same product language, then changes its pacing and interaction rules to fit the moment.

02
Private · after conflict

Repair slows the moment down

Repair begins with a body check-in, moves through grounding, then opens into AI-guided processing and an optional invitation to reconnect with a partner. In a charged moment, fewer choices help the experience feel supportive rather than clinical or task-driven.

  1. 01Body check-in
  2. 02Grounding
  3. 03AI conversation
  4. 04Reconnect invite
  5. 05Optional summary
03
Shared · active conflict

Resolve structures a difficult conversation

Resolve is a shared mode where both partners work through an active conflict with AI facilitating. The interaction needed to support fairness, consent, pacing, and context from both sides.

APartner A
AIFacilitator
BPartner B
  • Both partners consent before the session begins.
  • Turn-taking gives each person uninterrupted speaking time.
  • Prompts adapt to what both people have already said.
  • The session can pause or end without forcing a resolution.
  • Summaries and follow-up actions remain consent-based.
04
Shared · ongoing growth

Refine is designed for repeat use

Refine supports couples who are not in active conflict but want to keep working on the relationship. A one-off “start now” action was not enough, so the experience became a sustainable shared ritual.

  1. 01Pick a time
  2. 02Repeat weekly
  3. 03Set reminder
  4. 04Notify partner

Personalization & safety

Personalization needed to feel useful without becoming invisible or intrusive.

Assessments

Personalize the wider product

Attachment style, love language, conflict style, emotional sensitivities, and relationship history can improve later experiences. The value is not the quiz—it is how those results quietly shape future support.

  1. Intro
  2. Questions
  3. AI profile
  4. Result
  5. Consent for joint use
Anything shared between partners stays explicit and optional.
Safety

Know when AI should stop

Safety exists both as a standalone space and inside live sessions. When a disclosure is bigger than the product should handle, the AI pauses and directs the user toward appropriate real-world support.

  1. Sensitive disclosure
  2. Pause
  3. Check danger
  4. Surface local help
  5. Continue only if ready
Choose restraint over engagement.

Designing the system

The work had to survive a major implementation change—not just look complete.

Midway through the project, budget pressure led to a full development-team change. Documentation became a hard delivery requirement, and system thinking became the difference between a collection of screens and a product another team could continue building.

~169 frames organized into module-level prototypes and documentation

Key iterations

Session cards

Made ownership clearer

Replaced long labels with partner identity and “Scheduled by [Name]”.

Refine scheduling

Supported long-term use

Expanded one-off actions into repeat, reminder, reschedule, and notification states.

Assessment results

Protected privacy

Replaced manual sharing with consent-based use inside joint sessions.

Motion workflow

Protected tone and accuracy

Locked accurate screens first, then added motion to priority transitions.

Component process

Reduced duplication

Moved from ad-hoc creation to a screen audit and variant-first approach.

Outcome

Attune moved from a fragmented application toward a coherent product system with clearer emotional roles and safer AI behaviour.

01

Clearer product architecture

Private and shared experiences now follow one understandable model.

02

Emotionally appropriate UX

Each mode uses different pacing, tone, and interaction rules.

03

Responsible AI boundaries

Consent, privacy, session limits, and safety are built into the experience.

04

Scalable handoff

Reusable components and documentation made the product easier to continue building.

The strongest outcome was not one hero screen. It was making very different experiences feel like they belonged to the same product without removing the differences that made each one useful.

Reflection

The hardest question was not:

“What should the AI say?”

It was:

“What role should AI have here—and what should it not be allowed to do?”

Attune pushed me to think about AI as part of a wider interaction system. Privacy, consent, pacing, emotional safety, repeated use, and handoff mattered as much as the conversation itself.
