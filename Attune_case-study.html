The product

A relationship, not a user. Two people, sharing one guided space.

Attune helps two partners understand each other and grow their relationship. It centres on a set of guided session modes — Resolve, Repair, Reflect, and Refine — that meet couples in different moments, from working through an active conflict to keeping a healthy relationship growing over time.

Those sessions are personalised by a set of assessments — attachment style, love language, conflict style, relational style, emotional sensitivities, and relationship history — so the guidance fits each partner, not a generic template. Partners connect their accounts and meet in live sessions, by text or voice, with AI facilitating the conversation.

The client arrived with the product's modules and functionality already defined. Our job was not to invent the product, but to give it a new visual direction, make it feel homogeneous across every module, weave in a responsible safety layer, and turn it into something a development team could actually build.

Resolve

A live, real-time session for two partners with AI facilitating a calm, structured conversation — mutual consent to begin, gentle pacing, "is this what you meant?" checks, and a low-pressure way to close or schedule a follow-up.

Repair

Reconnecting after a conflict has happened

Opens with a body check-in and a short grounding moment, then an AI-guided conversation that helps a partner process what happened and reach out — including an invitation-to-reconnect flow and an optional shared summary. Every screen is paced and worded with care.

Why it matters: emotional intelligence applied to a genuinely delicate human moment.
Refine

Keeping a good relationship growing

Built around ongoing practice: schedule a session (date, time, weekly repeat, reminders), reschedule or suggest another time, and manage partner notifications. Sessions run as a three-way conversation — both partners and AI — by text or voice, closing with a summary both partners consent to save.

Why it matters: scheduling systems, multi-party conversation, and voice — high-value patterns most studios don't attempt.
Assessments

Personalising the whole product

Six short quizzes — attachment style, love language, conflict style, relational style, emotional sensitivities, and relationship history — each running end to end from intro to an AI-generated personal profile. Results are auto-delivered and, with consent, brought into joint sessions rather than left as a dead-end PDF.

Why it matters: plain data collection turned into an engaging, personalised input that feeds everything else.
Safety

Knowing when to hand off to real help

A standalone space of region-aware crisis resources (US, UK, Canada, Australia) and a vetted, non-ad-influenced therapist directory — plus an in-session safety net: the AI can pause a live session, surface crisis lines and an immediate-danger path, and let the user continue only when they're ready. Clear throughout that Attune is an educational tool, not a crisis service.

Why it matters: responsible, safety-first AI — a serious trust signal in a sensitive domain.
Design system

The shared foundation that made handover possible

A full component library — buttons and CTAs across states, input fields, chips (input, filter, choice, and suggestion; single- and multi-select), toggles, list items and cards, avatars, date and time pickers, session-reminder and notification cards, voice players with waveforms, tab bars, and plan cards. Built with variants rather than duplicates and themed per mode, so the system scales cleanly as new modules are added.

Why it matters: the difference between attractive screens and a product engineering can actually build and extend.
How AI is used

AI as a facilitator — never a replacement.

The design brief for the AI was restraint: help two people talk to each other better, and get out of the way. Every touchpoint below was shaped to soften and personalise human moments rather than automate them.

01

Facilitated live sessions

AI guides real-time conversations inside each mode, pacing the dialogue and keeping both partners heard.

02

Three-way conversations

Both partners and the AI share a single thread in joint sessions — a chat pattern designed for three voices, not two.

03

Gentle suggestion cards

"Let's try something" prompts appear in-line to move a stuck conversation forward without taking it over.

04

Voice sessions

Spoken input with a live waveform, so a couple can talk naturally and let the AI respond in kind.

05

Personalised from assessments

Assessment results feed the AI so its guidance fits each partner's attachment, conflict, and communication style.

06

AI-generated profiles

Raw quiz answers become a warm, readable personal profile a partner actually wants to read.

07

Message polishing

Free-form messages between partners can be refined into a respectful tone before they're sent.

08

Safety awareness

The AI recognises when something is beyond the app and pauses to surface real-world support — the most important thing it does.

How we worked

Messy inputs in. One coherent product out.

Direction for Attune lived everywhere — client calls, mail threads, Slack, Figma comments, and meeting recordings. Rather than react to it screen by screen, we made structure the discipline: turning scattered inputs into documented decisions, task lists, and a single source of truth the whole team could work from.

That included an AI-assisted workflow — pulling meeting transcripts into clear decisions and checklists, and maintaining an "organisation brain" that aggregated context from across the team's tools. On the craft side, we ran a screen-by-screen component audit: flag every repeating element, cross-reference before building anything new, and prefer variants over new components. It's what kept the system clean — and what let us hand the product to a brand-new development team without losing a beat.

Turning points

Two moments that defined the work.

Story 01 · Handover under fire

When the dev team changed overnight

Partway through, budget constraints led the client to replace their entire development team — with a hard deadline that every file be handover-ready within days. "Good design" was no longer enough; the work had to be legible to engineers who'd never seen the product. We shifted into a rigorous, module-by-module documentation effort across a ~169-screen Figma file, auditing every screen and packaging build-ready components. The pressure validated the system-first approach: the structure we'd been building is exactly what made a clean handoff possible.

Story 02 · Designing restraint

The moment the app must step back

The hardest question in a relationship app isn't what to do when things go well — it's what to do when someone reveals something bigger than the product should hold. We built a safety layer that lets the AI recognise those moments, gently pause the session, and surface real crisis support — while always leaving the user in control. Choosing to hand a person off to real-world help rather than keep them engaged is the decision that best captures how we think about responsibility in emotionally sensitive products.

Major iterations

What changed, and why.

01

Refine scheduling — from a one-off action to a repeatable ritual

A single "start now" wasn't enough for a growth mode. We built date/time selection, weekly repeat, reminders, rescheduling, and partner notifications. Why: to turn Refine into a habit two partners can keep in sync.

02

Safety — from a static resource page to an in-session net

Safety began as a standalone list. We extended it so support surfaces in context during a live session. Why: crisis moments don't wait for someone to navigate to a tab.

03

Assessment results — reworked around joint use

Removed the dead-end PDF download and manual share; results are auto-delivered and, with consent, brought into joint sessions. Softened copy and clearer choices throughout. Why: results should feel personal and shared-by-design, not transactional.

04

Session cards — from labels to people

Resolve and Refine cards now lead with the partner's avatar and "Scheduled by…" instead of a session name. Why: transparency between partners, and consistency across the card system.

05

Motion — sequenced after screens

Producing motion and pixel-accurate UI at once gave inaccurate results; we locked accurate screens first, then layered a handful of priority transitions. Why: to protect both visual accuracy and the calm tone the modes need.

06

Design system — from ad-hoc to a screen-by-screen audit

Components were built without a master list, causing duplication. We formalised a repeatable identification process. Why: a clean, maintainable system ready for handover.

The USP

Guidance that's specific to two people, and one moment.

Attune treats a relationship as something shared between two people and specific to them — adapting to each partner through assessments and to the moment through its modes, and using AI to make hard conversations gentler rather than to automate them away. And it knows when to stop: handing a person to real-world support is built into the product, not bolted on.
